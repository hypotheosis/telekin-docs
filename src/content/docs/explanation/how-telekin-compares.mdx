Most AI memory is either locked inside one app, or written as notes for you rather than for your AI. Telekin is a shared memory that your AIs read and write, but where you can see and undo every change.

What Telekin is strictest about is how safely an AI can write:

- **Drafts before anything goes live.** Edits sit in a draft until they are approved for publishing, and you can look first.
- **Provenance.** Each concept records who wrote it, when, and from what source.
- **History.** Every publish is kept, and any publish can be reverted.

## Built-in AI memory

The memory built into an AI app belongs to that app. What one app learns about you, the others never see, and you can't really inspect or version it. With Telekin, several apps can share one memory, and it's plain Markdown you can read and export.

## Auto-extracted memory tools

Tools in this family mostly pull out small facts automatically and store them in a database you aren't supposed to look at. Telekin stores structured notes instead: one per project, system, person or decision, with links between them. Your AI gets a coherent picture ("here's how this is set up and what broke last month") rather than loose snippets.

## Obsidian directly

Hooking an AI up to an Obsidian vault works, and it's a fine setup if you only use desktop tools. The differences:

- **Local only.** A vault lives on one machine. Telekin is a hosted server, so your memory isn't tied to the computer where your files sit.
- **Your notes stay yours.** An AI writing into your personal vault gets messy fast. Telekin keeps the AI's memory separate, reviewable and undoable, so it can write freely without trashing your own notes.

## Other Markdown-over-MCP tools

Other tools also keep Markdown notes and expose them to AI apps over MCP, the standard way AI apps connect to tools like Telekin. Some are more polished than Telekin today, and some work in more places. Where Telekin is deliberate is the write path: drafts first, provenance on every fact, and history you can revert. If you already use such a tool, compare how each one lets an AI change your notes, and how you would undo it.

**Phones:** Claude's mobile apps should pick up a connector you set up on the web or desktop. Other mobile apps aren't confirmed yet.

## What's planned

Telekin is early, and some things other tools have are not here yet:

- **Self-serve export** is planned. Today [export is on request](/explanation/your-data/).
- **Teams** work today, set up by us on request; teams managing their own members are planned.
- **A web view** of your memory in your Telekin console: browse and search what your AIs wrote.

For what works today, see [Connect your AI](/how-to/connect/). The best way to compare is to [try it](/start/first-15-minutes/): most people notice within a day or two whether their AI feels like it already knows them.
