When your AI app connects to Telekin, it gets a set of tools. You don't call them yourself: you talk to your AI, and it picks the right tool. This page lists them so you know what your AI is able to do, and what to expect it to ask permission for.

Your AI app may show these names with a prefix, such as `telekin` (the name you gave the server when you connected it). The full technical reference, with every parameter, is the [okf-mcp tool reference](https://github.com/hypotheosis/okf_mcp/blob/main/docs/reference/tools.md); Telekin runs on that open-source server.

When you ask your AI to remember something, Telekin tells it to save it to your memory rather than keep it in the chat, and that your asking is enough permission. Telekin also tells it to save things worth keeping that you share without being asked, such as a preference, a decision or a fact about yourself, and then to tell you which page it saved and what it says, so you can say if you don't want it kept. When you ask what your AI remembers, or refer to something you told it before, `search` tells it to look in your memory first.

## Finding things

| Tool | What it does for you |
|---|---|
| `search` | Looks for concepts (the pages in your memory) that match all of your words, and shows a short snippet of each hit. |
| `get_concept` | Reads one concept in full, or just one section of a long one. If the section isn't there, it lists the sections that are. |
| `read_document` | Reads a folder's contents page (`index.md`), the change log (`log.md`) or the catalog's house rules (`AGENTS.md`). |
| `list_catalogs` | Shows which catalogs your connection can reach, by name and display name, and which one is in use right now. |
| `whoami` | Says who the AI is acting for: your account, whether it signed in or uses a key, what it can reach, its default catalog, and Telekin's current time. |
| `cited_by` | Finds the concepts that cite a given source, such as a document or a web page. Asked with no source, it lists every source the memory cites and how many concepts cite each. |

## Writing (always as a draft first)

Nothing your AI writes is visible until it publishes. Until then it sits in a draft, called a changeset, that you can look over and throw away.

| Tool | What it does for you |
|---|---|
| `open_changeset` | Starts a draft. Every change goes into it. |
| `create_concept` | Adds a new concept to the draft. |
| `edit_document` | Changes a piece of text in an existing concept, by swapping exact wording. |
| `replace_body` | Rewrites the whole text of a concept, leaving its details at the top alone. |
| `set_frontmatter` | Changes one of a concept's descriptive details, such as its title or description. |
| `add_source` | Records where a fact came from, such as a document or a conversation. |
| `remove_source` | Removes a recorded source that is no longer cited. |
| `log` | Adds a dated line to the catalog's change log. |
| `index` | Lists a concept on its folder's contents page, or describes a folder on its parent's contents page. For a concept that's already listed, it changes that listing's one-line summary, for example to replace a placeholder description. |
| `pending_changes` | Shows exactly what the draft would change, line by line. |
| `lint` | Checks the draft, or the whole catalog, for mistakes such as broken links. |
| `publish` | Makes the draft real, with a short message saying what changed. |
| `discard` | Throws away some or all of a draft. Published concepts are untouched. |
| `list_changesets` | Finds your open drafts again, for example after a chat is lost. |

## Tracking where facts came from

| Tool | What it does for you |
|---|---|
| `stamp` | Marks a concept as written, or as checked against the real world, with who did it and when. |
| `review` | Records that a concept was reviewed and what was found, and can queue questions about it. |
| `question` | Queues a question on a concept, such as "is this still true?", without a full review. |
| `unquestion` | Removes a question once it has been answered. |
| `open_questions` | Lists every question waiting to be answered. |

## Keeping it tidy

| Tool | What it does for you |
|---|---|
| `sprawl` | Finds concepts that have grown too long or change too often, and suggests where to split them. |
| `links_report` | Finds broken links, orphan concepts that nothing links to, and places that mention a concept without linking to it. |
| `oldest` | Lists the concepts that have gone longest without being checked. |
| `move_concept` | Moves or renames a concept and fixes every link to it. |
| `split_concept` | Carves one section out of a long concept into a new concept of its own. |
| `delete_concept` | Deletes a concept. It refuses while anything still links to it. |
| `repair_indexes` | Fills in missing contents pages and listings, as a draft you publish like any other change. |

## History

| Tool | What it does for you |
|---|---|
| `history` | Lists every past version of a document, with the message, when, and who made it: the person whose account made the change, and the name tied to their key or connection. It can show only one person's changes, or only changes since a given time. It also gives the document's latest version number, which `revert` can check against. |
| `recent_changes` | Lists everything published across a memory, newest first, with the same details. It can narrow that to one person, or to changes since a given time. |
| `revert` | Brings back an earlier version, as a new draft that you publish like any other change. It can be told which version you last saw; if someone has published the document since, it refuses and changes nothing. This also guards bringing back a deleted document. |

Because every publish is kept in the history, an unwanted change can be undone. See [Review, undo and export](/how-to/guides/review-undo-export/).

## Feedback

| Tool | What it does for you |
|---|---|
| `send_feedback` | Sends feedback about Telekin (something broken, confusing, missing or good) to the Telekin team, through a third-party form service. Ask your AI to "send feedback to Telekin that...". It should ask you before including anything from your memory or personal details. |

## Maintenance

Tools whose names start with `maintenance_` are for Telekin's automated catalog checks. They need a special kind of key, so your AI can't use them, even though it may see them listed.

## How tools are used

Whether your AI app asks you before each tool runs depends on the app. Many apps ask the first time and let you choose "always allow".

Telekin tells your app what kind each tool is, and some apps use that to decide when to ask:

- **Reading tools**, such as `search`, `get_concept`, `history` and `recent_changes`, only look.
- **Tools that add something**, such as `open_changeset`, `create_concept`, `add_source`, `log` and `question`, add to a draft without changing what's already there.
- **Tools that change or remove existing content** are marked that way: `edit_document`, `replace_body`, `set_frontmatter`, `stamp`, `review`, `index`, `repair_indexes`, `remove_source`, `unquestion`, `revert`, `move_concept` and `split_concept`, as well as `delete_concept`, `discard` and `publish`. An app that asks before such tools will ask for these.
- **`send_feedback`** is marked as reaching outside Telekin, since it sends your message to a form service.

Apart from `publish`, and `discard` (which throws away part of a draft), the tools listed above as changing content only change your draft. Nothing in your memory changes until your AI publishes.
